# Amidst privacy policy

Effective November 25, 2026.

Amidst is an independent, open-source client for iPhone and Android for the Zood Location network, maintained through the [iOS project](https://github.com/Emphasize5599/family-locator-ios) and the [Android project](https://github.com/Emphasize5599/george/tree/family-ios-compat). It is not affiliated with Tangled Wires, LLC. The account and encrypted-message service is operated by Zood at `api.zood.xyz`; its [mobile-app privacy policy](https://www.zood.xyz/privacy/mobile-apps) also applies to that service.

## Information used

- **Account information:** your username, public account identifier and public encryption key are sent to the Zood service. You may optionally provide an email address at registration. Password-derived encryption protects your private keys; the app does not send your plaintext password to the service.
- **Location and device information:** when you allow location access, the app obtains coordinates, timestamp, accuracy, speed and direction, and may include battery level, charging state, device platform, whether location requests are supported, and a movement category such as walking or driving when you allow motion or physical activity access. Location packages are encrypted on your device for the people you choose to share with. The service carries and stores encrypted packages; recipients can decrypt them.
- **Nicknames:** an optional shared nickname is sent inside encrypted messages to friends and kept in your encrypted account backup. A private nickname you choose for a friend stays on your account and in its encrypted backup; it is never sent to that friend. Names do not change usernames or account identity.
- **Linked contacts:** on iPhone, the system picker provides the contact you select. If you allow Contacts access to restore photos, Amidst looks up thumbnails by saved contact identifier for friends you already linked. On Android, granting Contacts access lets Amidst list and search your address book in the picker and read the name and photo for the contact you select. The app doesn't upload the address book. The selected display name and contact reference can be restored from your end-to-end encrypted account backup. The contact photo stays on the device. A contact link is never sent to that friend.
- **One device at a time:** your encrypted account backup records which device you most recently signed in on (a random device ID, the platform and the time). Other devices see this and sign themselves out locally; nothing on your account is deleted.
- **Friend bar arrangement:** the order of people in the friend bar and any folders you make are kept on this device and in your end-to-end encrypted account backup. They only organize the bar and never change who can see your location.
- **Friends and profile photo:** the app stores your friends, sharing settings, received locations and optional profile photo locally. It uploads an encrypted friends-list backup to your account, including small thumbnails of shared friend profile photos already on your device, and sends your selected profile photo in encrypted friend requests and to friends you share with. Photo selection uses the system picker or a camera action you choose. Amidst does not request access to your entire photo library.
- **Service connections:** the Zood service receives the requests needed to authenticate, check usernames during account creation and Add Friend, and deliver encrypted data. As with any internet service, it receives connection information such as an IP address. The independent app maintainers do not control Zood's infrastructure or its retention of service logs.
- **Maps and address lookup:** iPhone uses Apple Maps and Core Location. Android uses MapLibre, the map style and tile providers configured by Zood, and Android’s geocoding service, if available. Map requests can reveal the area being viewed, and address lookup can send coordinates to the device’s geocoding service. These services have their own privacy policies.

The app contains no advertising or third-party analytics SDK and does not use advertising identifiers or cross-app tracking. Local diagnostic logs describe connection/location-service events, not raw coordinates, passwords or encryption keys.

## Your choices and consent

You can view friends without sharing your own location. Add Friend gives you a separate choice to share your location or send a request without it. An optional one-time invite link lets the first recipient connect when the inviter’s app processes the acceptance. Sharing with new friends is off by default. If you turn it on in onboarding or Settings, people you explicitly approve can receive your location. A one-time link you create while sharing is on can also grant access to the person who redeems it while the setting is on. The choice is saved on this device for your account. You can turn off sharing with any friend from their card. The inviter’s device saves the link token and includes its redemption state in the next end-to-end encrypted account backup. Invitations can include the profile photo and nickname you selected. Turn off a friend’s sharing toggle or remove the friend to stop future sharing with them. Other people may retain locations or screenshots they previously received.

Allow All the Time on Android or Always on iPhone enables background location sharing. Live Requests uses background location activity to remain available for requests and may use additional battery. You can switch it off in the app's Settings, change location permission in the device’s Settings, or log out. Logging out removes this app's credentials and local friend data and stops its location activity on this device; it does not delete your server account.

Timed sharing creates a link containing a decryption key. Anyone with that link can view the shared location. Do not post the link publicly unless you intend that access. Stopping the share or letting its timer expire stops future updates; the last transmitted location and copies already received may remain accessible. The app cannot recall screenshots or data retained by recipients.

Pending requests can display the sender’s chosen profile photo and name. Their location becomes visible after you add or approve them and they choose to share. If they share later, it updates the existing friendship. You can choose separately whether to share back. Deny dismisses the request. Block removes an existing friendship and prevents new requests from appearing until you unblock. Neither action sends a notification about the decision. Amend blocks in Settings; Amidst enforces them on devices that restore your encrypted account backup. Previously delivered locations may remain on the recipient’s device.

## Storage, retention and deletion

On iPhone, account credentials and private keys are stored in the Keychain, and the friends file uses iOS file protection. On Android, the app stores its account and local friendship data in app-private storage. Pending requests and blocked-account identifiers are stored per account and are removed from this device after successful account deletion. Blocked accounts are also kept in your end-to-end encrypted account backup, so blocks return when you sign in again; pending requests are not backed up. The app uses its own preferences for settings and upload timing. Server backups and location payloads are encrypted, but encryption does not conceal all account identifiers or connection metadata.

## Delete your Amidst account

Use **Settings > Account > Delete Account** to permanently delete the authenticated Zood account and its encrypted friends-list backup. This affects the same account in other Zood-compatible clients. After the server confirms deletion, this app signs out, removes local account data, and stops location monitoring. An unsuccessful network request is shown as an error; logging out alone is not represented as deletion. You can also [request deletion on the Zood website](https://www.zood.xyz/location/delete-account) without reinstalling Amidst. Amidst and Zood-compatible apps use the same account.

The app does not promise to erase encrypted packages, provider backups or logs beyond the deletion behavior documented by the service operator. Previously received locations, shared links and copies saved by other people cannot be recalled.

## Contact and changes

For Amidst support, privacy questions, or abuse reports, open an issue on the project's [GitHub Issues page](https://github.com/Emphasize5599/family-locator-ios/issues). **GitHub issues are public**, and GitHub handles your account and anything you post under its own privacy policy. Report a Problem in the app opens the GitHub Issues page; you can read it without an account, and filing an issue requires a GitHub account. Nothing is sent automatically, and no location, photo, password or encryption key is attached. Do not post passwords, location coordinates, private keys, timed-sharing links, or anything else you don't want public. Reports cannot directly suspend an account on the independently operated Zood service; backend concerns may require contacting its operator. For private account or server-data questions, use [Zood's contact page](https://www.zood.xyz/contact).

Policy changes will be published on this page with an updated effective date. Material changes to data use require updated disclosures and, where applicable, consent.
